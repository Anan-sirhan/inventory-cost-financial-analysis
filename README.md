# Inventory Cost and Financial Performance Analysis

## Project Overview

Inventory management directly affects a company's working capital, product availability, operating costs, and customer satisfaction.

This Excel portfolio project transforms simulated inventory data into practical financial and operational insights. The analysis identifies inventory shortage risks, evaluates cost exposure, compares Budget COGS with Actual COGS, and highlights the categories and suppliers requiring management attention.

## Business Questions

* Which products are exposed to a high shortage risk?
* What is the estimated quantity and financial value of the shortage?
* How does Actual COGS compare with Budget COGS?
* Which product categories contribute most to the unfavorable cost variance?
* Which categories and suppliers represent the highest shortage exposure?
* What do the estimated Inventory Turnover and Days Inventory Outstanding indicate?

## Key Findings

* 1,604 out of 3,000 products were classified as high risk, representing 53.47% of the analyzed products.
* The estimated shortage reached approximately 4.76 million units.
* The estimated total shortage value was approximately 1.223 billion currency units.
* Monthly Actual COGS was approximately 1.600 billion, compared with Budget COGS of approximately 1.518 billion.
* The unfavorable monthly COGS variance was approximately 81.71 million, or 5.38% above budget.
* GPU and Cooling were the main cost variance drivers, together representing approximately 45.9% of the total variance.
* Based on the available inventory snapshot, Estimated Inventory Turnover was 9.81 and Estimated Days Inventory Outstanding was 37.19 days.

## Business Recommendations

* Investigate the products and suppliers contributing most to shortage exposure.
* Review purchasing prices and terms in categories with significant unfavorable cost variances.
* Prioritize GPU and Cooling for a detailed SKU-level and supplier-level investigation.
* Monitor inventory turnover by category rather than relying only on the overall company result.
* Compare the estimated turnover metrics with historical periods once beginning and ending inventory data become available.
* Improve inventory allocation to reduce both shortage risk and excess inventory.

## Dashboard

The dashboard provides a management-level view of:

* High-risk products and high-risk rate
* Total shortage quantity and value
* Estimated annual COGS
* Unfavorable monthly COGS variance
* Budget vs. Actual COGS by category
* Shortage value by category
* Shortage value by supplier
* Estimated Inventory Turnover and Days Inventory Outstanding

## Methodology Note

Estimated Annual COGS was calculated by annualizing the available monthly demand-based Actual COGS.

Estimated Inventory Turnover and Estimated Days Inventory Outstanding are based on the available inventory snapshot. Historical average inventory data was not available, so these metrics should be interpreted as estimates rather than audited financial KPIs.
